================================================================================
SCENARIO 1 - THE RULES, IN PLAIN ENGLISH
================================================================================
This lists every constraint the optimiser uses. Each rule is tagged
with its model equation number and marked:
  [HARD] = can never be broken (physics / safety),
  [SOFT] = a goal we try to hit; the plan may miss it at a penalty cost (so the
           planner can never get "stuck" with no valid plan), or
  [S1]   = a Scenario-1 implementation choice / adaptation of the base model.

HOW SCENARIO 1 RUNS (context for the rules below):
The controller is a MULTI-DAY, CROSS-DAY RECEDING HORIZON. The plan is re-solved every
15 minutes, and each window spans the rest of TODAY plus `lookahead_days` future daytime
blocks (global interval index g = (day-1)*n_day + k), so the plan always "sees" tomorrow.
We simulate `n_days` reported days PLUS one extra BUFFER day that is dropped from all
outputs (it just gives the last kept day full lookahead). Each day has TWO PHASES:
  * PHASE 1 - daytime MPC (08:00-18:00 per day): all the constraints below are solved
    together for the CEV work and the MCS routing/charging.
  * PHASE 2 - overnight (18:00-08:00, EVERY night): a simple, deterministic "smart-charge"
    that refills the MCS in the cheapest hours, closing the MCS nightly cycle (8a).
Across a night the MCS is recharged to full + parked at the grid, while the CEV battery
and any unfinished work CARRY OVER unchanged into the next morning.
Productive work happens 08:00-12:00 and 14:00-17:00, with a 12:00-14:00 lunch.


================================================================================
OBJECTIVE (Eq. 1) - what the planner minimises
================================================================================
The cheapest overall plan, adding up:
  - (1) ENERGY cost: electricity bought from the grid, priced by time of day;
  - (1) CARBON cost: optional $/ton on the grid CO2 of that electricity;
  - (1) DEMAND charges: a whole-day (non-coincident) peak charge plus an extra
        on-peak (16:00-21:00) peak charge;
  - (1) MISSED-WORK penalty: rho_miss per hour of unfinished work (the model's own
        soft slack from 12c);
  - (1) MCS TOWING labour: rho_labor per hour the MCS spends being towed between
        nodes (discourages excessive shuttling).
  The objective is EXACTLY Eq. (1): there are NO extra tuning penalties. Precedence
  (12d), travel pacing (13) and the CEV energy-neutral terminal (8b) are enforced as
  HARD constraints (see below), not as objective penalties.


================================================================================
A. CHARGING & DISCHARGING POWER OF THE MCSs AND CEVs  (Eqs. 2-4)
================================================================================
(2) [HARD] Each MCS's total charge / discharge power is the sum of its per-node
    charging (at grid nodes) and discharging (at construction sites) (2a-2b); it
    charges ONLY at grid nodes and discharges ONLY at sites (2c-2d); power leaving an
    MCS at a site equals the total delivered to the CEVs plugged in there (2e); all
    powers are non-negative (2f).

(3) [HARD] An MCS can draw grid power only where it is physically present, capped by
    its charge rate (3a-3b); only ONE MCS may use a single grid connection per
    interval (3c); site discharge is capped by the discharge rate and allowed only
    where the MCS is present (3d).

(4) [HARD] Power into a CEV through one plug is capped, and zero unless that CEV is
    plugged into that MCS (4a); a CEV's total intake is capped by its accept rate and
    allowed only while it is in charging mode (4b); an MCS can charge at most n_plug
    CEVs at once (4c).


================================================================================
B. ENERGY CONSUMPTION & STATE OF ENERGY  (Eqs. 5-8)
================================================================================
(5) [HARD] A CEV's work power is bounded by its work capacity and is zero outside
    productive hours (5a-5c); in any interval it does at most one activity and is
    either working OR charging, never both (5d); its work power equals the chosen
    activity's draw (5e).
    [S1] productive hours = 08:00-12:00 and 14:00-17:00 (the 12:00-14:00 lunch and
    17:00-18:00 wind-down are excluded; the CEV idles then, and may charge).

(6) [HARD] Driving consumes a fixed energy per in-transit interval, summed over arcs
    into the MCS's travel energy (6a-6b).

(7) [HARD] Battery bookkeeping: each MCS / CEV next-SOE = SOE now + energy charged in
    - energy discharged / worked out - travel loss (7a-7b).

(8) [HARD] ENERGY NEUTRAL: every battery ends the FULL CYCLE at the level it started,
    SOE_end = SOE_ini (8a-8b); and SOE always stays within [min, max], floor = 20% of
    capacity (8c-8d).
    [S1] In the multi-day horizon the CEV battery FLOWS across days - it is NOT forced
    back to its start level every evening. The "return to start" rule (8b) is applied
    ONLY when a window reaches the TRUE horizon end (the buffer day's 18:00), written as
    the one-sided inequality SOE_CEV[end] >= SOE_CEV_ini - term_tol. The upper side is
    already the SOE_max bound, so with term_tol = 0 this is the EXACT equality. A SMALL
    default margin term_tol = 0.1 kWh (~0.7% of pack) absorbs the online estimator's
    certainty-equivalent drift (planned vs. true powers); with a zero-margin equality that
    drift can make the re-plan INFEASIBLE. There is still NO reactive fallback: if even this
    margin is exceeded the window is reported INFEASIBLE and the plant HOLDS state.
    [S1] The MCS is treated differently: within a day it draws from the grid only as needed
    to respect its 20% floor; the SOE link is BROKEN across each night and the MCS is reset
    to full (SOE_MCS_ini) by the Phase-2 overnight smart-charge - one refill PER NIGHT.


================================================================================
C. SPATIAL ROUTING & CONNECTION STATUS OF THE MCSs  (Eqs. 9-11)
================================================================================
(9) [HARD] A CEV may connect at a node only if it is assigned there (9a), and only if
    an MCS is physically present at that node (9b).

(10) [HARD] No travel from a node to itself (10a); the in-transit flag follows from
     the departure decision and the arc travel time (10b); at each instant an MCS is
     parked at exactly one node OR in transit on exactly one arc (10c); each MCS
     starts each day at a grid node (10d); and ENDS EVERY daytime block parked
     at a grid node - "home" by each 18:00, ready for that night's overnight recharge (10e).

(11) [HARD] Arrival / departure bookkeeping ties presence to the trips taken with the
     travel-time delay (11a-11c); total arrivals = total departures over the horizon
     (11d); a node cannot be both arrived at and departed from in one interval (11e).
     [S1] the plan also starts the MCS exactly where it really is now (parked, or
     part-way through a trip it already began).


================================================================================
D. OPERATIONAL ADJUSTMENT FOR CEV WORK SCHEDULING  (Eqs. 12-13)
================================================================================
(12) Each CEV does at most one sub-activity per interval, only at the site it is
     assigned to (12a-12b) [HARD]; all required digging / loading hours per site
     should be done, any shortfall being "missed work" at the rho_miss penalty (12c)
     [SOFT - this is the model's own slack]. [S1] work is a PER-DAY SCHEDULE, not a single
     lumpsum: each reported day has its OWN per-site dig/load quota (from work_by_day.csv,
     or the place.csv default repeated). The quota is a CUMULATIVE target: that day's hours
     arrive each morning and any shortfall ROLLS OVER (and is re-penalised) into the next
     day, so leftover work is carried, never faked or lost. The dropped buffer day gets NO
     fresh work (wind-down only).
     PRECEDENCE - cumulative loading cannot
     outpace a fixed multiple (scale) of cumulative digging (12d) [HARD], evaluated WITHIN
     each day-block (the counters restart each morning); REST RULE -
     over any rolling (t_limit+15 min)
     window a CEV does at most t_limit hours of construction work, i.e. >=1 idle break
     interval per hour, and the break may be used to charge (12e) [HARD].
     NOTE: t_limit is a USER INPUT (no fixed numeric value). It is ENABLED here with
     t_limit_rest = 1 (>=1 idle break per rolling 5-interval / 75-min window); set
     t_limit_rest large (e.g. 999) to disable the rest rule. [S1] the rest rule is SEEDED
     from the current day's applied Work/Break history (a seam constraint), so the limit
     holds ACROSS the every-15-min re-solves, not just inside one window.
     [S1] across the daytime the CEV is always doing exactly one of dig / load /
     travel / idle (idle is the residual, and is when it may charge).

(13) [HARD] TRAVEL PACING: a CEV's cumulative repositioning (traveling) is tied to
     its cumulative productive work - about one travel interval per kappa_wt (= 4)
     productive intervals - as a two-sided band (13a-13b).


================================================================================
E. ADDITIONAL CONSTRAINTS IN THE MODEL  (present in Scenario_1.jl, but not one of
   the numbered equations 1-13)
================================================================================
These are real constraints in the solved MILP. They either realise an objective
term from Eq. (1), or handle the closed-loop (every-15-min re-solve) reality, or are
optional switches.

(E1) [HARD] PEAK-DEMAND TRACKERS. Two variables track the grid peak that the demand
     charges in Eq. (1) are priced on: a whole-day (non-coincident) peak and an
     on-peak (16:00-21:00) peak; each must be >= the total grid draw in every
     (relevant) interval. [S1] they are also seeded with the realised peak so far, so
     the demand charge reflects the WHOLE day across the re-solves.

(E2) [HARD, optional] GRID-DRAW CAP. An extra hard cap on total grid power per
     interval (`peak_demand_limit`) can be switched on; it is OFF by default.

(E3) [HARD] CLOSED-LOOP CARRY-IN. Each 15-min re-solve is initialised from the REAL
     measured state: every MCS/CEV starting SOE equals its measured value; the MCS
     starts exactly where it physically is (parked at its node, OR part-way through a
     trip already begun, which is forced to continue to completion); and completed
     work and daily peaks so far are carried in. (This is the MPC form of the
     initial conditions in 7-8 and 10d.)

(E4) [HARD] CHARGE-ONLY-WHILE-IDLE LINK. A CEV may be in charging mode only in an
     interval whose chosen activity is "idle" - the implementation link behind the
     work-or-charge exclusivity of (5d).

(E5) [HARD, optional] SITE-VISIT SWITCHES. `require_site_visit` (each MCS must visit
     at least one site) and `single_visit_per_site` (at most one arrival + one
     departure per site) can be enabled; both are OFF in the shipped model.

(E6) [S1] NO FALLBACK. The model is HARD by default (Eq. 1 objective; precedence 12d,
     pacing 13 and the CEV terminal 8b as hard constraints), with only missed-work (12c)
     carrying the model's own slack. If a 15-min re-plan is infeasible from a drifted
     realised state, NOTHING is relaxed automatically: that window is reported INFEASIBLE
     and the plant HOLDS state (no work / no charging) for that interval. The count of
     infeasible/held windows is reported at the end of a run. Relaxation is available only
     MANUALLY via the run_scenario_1 kwargs soft_prec / soft_pace / soft_term (all default
     false = hard).

(E7) [HARD] CEV KEEP-UP RESERVE (recursive feasibility of the terminal 8b). A CEV is only
     charged while the MCS is parked at its site, and the MCS must leave for a grid node
     before the horizon end (evening end-at-grid rule); after it leaves the CEV only idles,
     and because idling itself draws power the SOE strictly DRAINS. So the terminal target
     (SOE_ini - term_tol) can become unreachable if the CEV is left too low too late. The
     reserve lower-bounds each CEV's SOE at every boundary by the least level from which the
     terminal is still reachable - built backward from the buffer day's 18:00 using the NET
     charge rate (max charge minus idle draw) and the last interval the MCS can still charge.
     It only binds in the final tail (inside the dropped buffer day), never distorting the
     reported days, and makes the hard terminal recursively feasible instead of a knife-edge.
     Result: both datasets run with ZERO infeasible/held windows.


================================================================================
SCENARIO-1 NOTES (design choices and adaptations)
================================================================================
- MULTI-DAY, CROSS-DAY: each 15-min window spans the rest of today + `lookahead_days`
  future daytime blocks. `n_days` reported days are simulated plus one dropped buffer day.
- TWO-PHASE solve PER DAY: a daytime MPC (Phase 1, all equations above) plus a
  deterministic overnight smart-charge (Phase 2) that closes the MCS cycle (8a) each night.
- Nights: the MCS SOE link is broken and reset to full each night; the CEV battery and any
  unfinished work carry over. The CEV "return to start" (8b) fires only at the horizon end.
- Productive hours are split by a lunch break; both fleets use a 20% SOE floor.

The model is HARD by default (objective = Eq. 1; precedence 12d, pacing 13, CEV terminal
8b all hard) with NO automatic fallback. When a 15-min re-plan cannot satisfy the hard
constraints from the realised state, that window is reported INFEASIBLE and the plant
simply HOLDS state (no work / no charging) for it - the constraints are honoured exactly
whenever the realised state allows, and never relaxed silently when it does not.
