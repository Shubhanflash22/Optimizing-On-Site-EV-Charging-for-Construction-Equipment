Initial-SOE sensitivity sweep — :input case

The CEV initial state-of-energy SOE_CEV_ini is swept across 10 values from the battery minimum (2.96 kWh) to its maximum (14.80 kWh). The receding-horizon closed loop was re-run for each value with no solver time limit (every 15-min window solved to the MIP gap) and a fixed seed, so differences come only from the start SOE. In this model SOE_CEV_ini is also the end-of-day floor target (the CEV must finish at or above it), so each point shifts the start level and its terminal target together.

Optimality is reported two ways: (i) feasibility — zero infeasible windows and both terminal targets met (MCS back to 250.00 kWh, CEV ≥ its start); and (ii) cost — the realised end-of-day total operating cost vs the ideal cost of the very first 08:00 whole-day plan. Actions changed counts the 15-min intervals whose realised activity differs from that first 08:00 plan, per unit (CEV1 and the MCS), out of 96 intervals each.

Headline findings

  • Feasibility: all 10 runs optimal & feasible (0 infeasible windows; every terminal target met).
  • Cheapest run: SOE_ini = 12.17 kWh at realised total $193.84.
  • Most plan changes: SOE_ini = 12.17 kWh with 40 changed intervals (CEV1 21 + MCS 19).
  • Fewest plan changes: SOE_ini = 5.59 kWh with 19 changed intervals.
  • Average plan changes across runs: 32.6 intervals (out of 192 = 2×96).

Interpretation

  • Cost is dominated by the missed-work penalty (rho_miss = $2000/h in this dataset): realised cost clusters into bands set by whole 0.25 h steps of missed work (0 h → ~$192, 0.25 h → ~$690, 0.5 h → ~$1185).
  • Charge threshold for zero missed work: for SOE_ini ≥ 8.22 kWh (≥ 44% of range) missed work is 0 and cost settles at its ~$192 minimum; below it the excavator starts too depleted to keep up before the MCS can recharge it.
  • Robustness (plan vs actual): at high SOE realised ≈ planned (|Δ| ≤ $1.6). At low/mid SOE the certainty-equivalent plan is fragile — the stochastic plant over-draws on some intervals and tips marginal cases into unplanned missed work (worst Δ = +$995.34 at SOE 6.91 kWh).
  • Non-monotonicity at low SOE is a stochastic-plant effect, not a model error: near the missed-work boundary, exactly which intervals the plant over-draws decides whether a penalty fires.
  • Actions changed does not track cost: it stays ~16–23% of intervals across the whole range; at high SOE these are cost-neutral re-timings of charging/idle, at low SOE similar counts are cost-relevant, so change-count must be read together with Δcost.
  • Recommended operating point: start each CEV at ≥ ~65% SOE to guarantee zero missed work and a robust (plan ≈ actual) schedule.
