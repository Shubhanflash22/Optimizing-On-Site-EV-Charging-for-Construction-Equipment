### 1. `Part 8.py` (Data Preparation)

The goal is to turn a raw, messy construction video into consistent tiny learning materials. First, the script forces the video to run at a strict speed of **25 frames per second**. Even if the original camera recorded faster or slower, this script resamples time so the movement always looks consistent. This is crucial because the AI learns "speed" relative to this heartbeat. The script uses a helper (a separate object detection tool called YOLO) to scan every single frame. It asks, "Is there an excavator here?" If the confidence is below **50%**, it throws that frame away. This ensures the AI doesn't study empty dirt. The AI can't watch an hour-long movie at once. It learns in short bursts. The script cuts the video into tiny clips, each exactly **16 frames** long (less than a second). It doesn't just cut end-to-end. It takes a clip, slides forward just **3 frames**, and cuts again. This creates massive overlap, generating thousands of practice examples from a single event, ensuring the AI sees the action from every possible starting point. The result is a library of thousands of perfectly timed, 16-frame mini-movies, sorted into folders like "Digging" and "Loading."

### 2. `Part 9.py` (Training the AI)

The goal is to teach the 3D ResNet model to recognize activities by studying the clips from Part 8. The AI is taught to distinguish between **5 specific activities**: Digging, Loading, Swinging, Travelling, and Idling. The AI doesn't study one clip at a time; that’s too slow. It studies **16 clips** simultaneously to spot patterns faster. To prevent the AI from cheating (like memorizing "the digging clip always has a blue sky"), the script randomly alters the clips during study. It might flip the image horizontally, darken the colors, or slightly tilt (shear) the frame. This forces the AI to learn the *motion* of the arm, not the background scenery. The script holds back **20%** of the videos. The AI is never allowed to study these. At the end of training, it is tested on this unseen footage to prove it can generalize to new days and weather conditions. The result is a trained model (`.pth`) that knows exactly what an excavator looks like when it digs, swings, or loads.

### 3. `Part 10.py` (Inference & Productivity)

The goal is to take the trained model and use it to predict on unknown data. The script processes the video in a single continuous pipeline that starts by forcing the raw footage to run at exactly **25 frames per second** so the AI sees movement at the correct speed. It then feeds the video into the AI in small chunks of **16 frames** at a time, asking the model to guess the activity for each moment. Because the AI can get twitchy and change its mind too fast, the script applies a cleanup rule that assumes every real action lasts at least **2 seconds**, checking a group of **50 frames** at once and letting the majority vote decide the final label. Once the timeline is smooth, it hunts for "cycles" by measuring the exact time from the start of one "Digging" phase to the start of the next one, counting every completed loop. Finally, it calculates a productivity score by taking that cycle count, projecting how many would finish in an hour, and multiplying that number by **1.5 cubic yards** (the assumed bucket size) to report exactly how much earth the machine moves per hour. The result is a second-by-second timeline of the day and a productivity report card for the site manager.
